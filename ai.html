Why now

Regulators and boards are asking for evidence, not intent.

From 10 December 2026, Australian privacy policies must describe decisions made or informed by computer programs that significantly affect individuals’ rights or interests. Overseas contractors who receive personal information also create cross-border obligations under APP 8. Boards need a register, rules and records that show the controls work.

Frameworks we work to
  • Privacy Act 1988, including automated decision-making transparency and APP 8
  • NIST AI Risk Management Framework: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage
  • ISO/IEC 42001 AI management system, so clients can later pursue certification
  • Singapore Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI

Kybern prepares clients for certification. A certification body audits them.

The Kybern autonomy scale

How much may the AI do on its own?

A shared vocabulary for every proposal, contract and report. It lets a board set the limit, then lets the evidence show the limit is respected.

L0

Assist

Drafts or answers. A person decides and acts.

L1

Recommend

Proposes an action. A person approves each one.

L2

Act with approval gates

Acts on low-risk steps. High-risk steps need approval.

L3

Act with sampling

Acts alone. People review a sample and every exception.

L4

Exception reporting

Acts alone. People review exceptions only.

R1

Low risk

Internal use, no personal information, reversible, no decisions about people. Register entry, owner and annual review.

R2

Moderate risk

Uses personal information or faces customers, with a person able to review. Adds an evaluation set and quarterly review.

R3

High risk

Significantly affects individuals, uses sensitive information, or takes irreversible actions. Adds approval gates, disclosure link and board visibility.
